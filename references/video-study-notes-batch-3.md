# Video study notes — batch 3

## Lesson 26: colour

- separates hue, saturation, and lightness;
- diagnoses dark/dull, glaring, monotonous, and conflicting palettes;
- repairs layered scientific objects through coherent warm/cool, analogous, and contrast systems;
- supports semantic hue with hierarchy controlled by saturation/lightness.

## Lesson 28: abstraction

- scientific drawing aims to communicate rather than reproduce reality;
- 3D is justified only when more intuitive or informative;
- core rule: remove information noise and strengthen central information.

## Lesson 32: gradient sharpening 2

- constructs BCC cells using clipped/partial atoms;
- constructs latitude/longitude spheres from repeated curves;
- constructs orthogonal electromagnetic-wave components;
- uses hard gradient stops/path splitting to manage crossings and occlusion.

## Lesson 36: 3D beakers and test tubes

- transparent vessels require separate shell, liquid, rim, meniscus, front/back parts, and freeform profiles;
- transparency is applied after geometry and Z-order are correct.

## Lesson 37: thirteen arrows

- final taxonomy: five line arrows, four planar arrows, four 3D arrows;
- construction combines line endpoints, freeform editing, booleans, repetition, depth, bevel, material, and lighting;
- arrow family must follow scientific meaning.

## Lesson 38: 3D molecular models

- compares ChemOffice, MolView, PowerPoint 3D models, and ThreeD MOL import;
- version support varies;
- imported 3D models may rotate but remain non-editable;
- a ThreeD-style MOL conversion can create native editable ball-and-stick objects;
- native generation must preserve molecular coordinates/connectivity and must not infer unreported chemistry.
